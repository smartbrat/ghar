Rule — inline in display type (baseline-grounded)

A canonical shape set inside a display headline is typography, not decoration — a noun standing in the sentence. Use a canonical path + its canonical colour only (clean filled vessel — never the billboard door/window variant). It must sit on the text baseline, grounded like a house on the ground — never vertically centred or floating. Size it ~1.05–1.1em (a touch taller than the caps), then nudge it down slightly so it settles into the line instead of towering. With two or more shapes, spread them apart — a different x-position on each line; never stack them at the same x on adjacent lines.

.headline      { line-height: 1.3 }              /* room so a raised glyph clears the line above */
.headline .t-line { display: block; white-space: nowrap }
.q-shape {
  display: inline-block; width: 1.08em; height: 1.08em;
  vertical-align: baseline;        /* sit ON the baseline (Clickl-style) */
  position: relative; top: .12em;  /* nudge down so it's grounded, not towering */
}
Copy the shapes

The nine silhouettes above, as copy-ready SVG. Paste one straight into a page and it renders with no build step, no sprite sheet and no dependency.

Copy puts the markup on your clipboard. SVG saves it as a file. PNG renders a transparent 1024 square in the colour this card is named for. The snippets carry no colour of their own: currentColor means a shape takes the color of whatever you put it inside, so one snippet serves every palette entry.

The family rule — why every shape carries a stroke

Eight of the nine draw a hard-cornered polygon, then round it by painting a 28-unit stroke in the same colour as the fill, with stroke-linejoin="round". The stroke is not an outline — it is the soft edge, and it expands the silhouette past the path, which is why the geometry stops at y=170 but the shape sits on y=184. Fill and stroke are both currentColor, so one color value drives the pair and they can never drift apart. If you replace them with literal hex values, they must match exactly or the edge will fringe.

Chimney Cottage (6) is the exception. Its curves are baked into the path, so it carries no stroke and needs none.

⚠
These cannot be exported to PNG A masked photo is two things: the shape, which lives in the markup, and the photograph, which lives at a URL. When an SVG is loaded as an image (which is what SVGOMG, a PNG converter and every "save as image" button do underneath) the browser runs it in a mode that fetches nothing at all, so the photo never arrives and you get an empty shape or an outright failure. A local path does not help; the block is on loading anything, not on the domain.

Two ways through. To ship a masked photo on a page, use the markup exactly as it is here, since a live page loads the image normally. To ship one as a flat file, composite it in an image editor and export a PNG, or embed the photograph in the SVG as a data: URI, which makes the file self-contained but very large.

The nine plain shapes in 9 Canonical Shapes have no such limit: they reference nothing, so their Copy, SVG and PNG buttons all work.

Restraint

Shapes support.
They don't dominate.

The nine shapes are a vocabulary, not a wallpaper. Use them at roughly 5% of any page view — one per section, never all at once. Ghar.tv must look diverse, not monolithic.

Avoid — Overloaded

Homes worth talking about.

When shapes appear in every heading, every card, every corner, the brand becomes monotone. The shapes lose their power and the platform stops looking diverse.

Preferred — Restrained

Homes worth talking about.

One shape per section. Let whitespace, typography, and photography do the heavy lifting. The shape becomes a punctuation mark — not the sentence.

01
The 5% Rule
Shapes occupy roughly 5% of any given page view — the same discipline as brand red. They accent, not dominate.
02
One Per Section
A section can have one inline shape, OR a masked photo, OR an accent mark. Never all three. Pick the single most impactful use.
03
Diversity Over Identity
Ghar.tv must look like a diverse platform, not "the shapes brand." Content, typography, and photography carry the identity — shapes support it.

Rule 03 · CTA Tiers

Two CTA tiers.
One per role.

Every CTA on the site belongs to one of two tiers — and they don't swap. Section main CTA is a bordered pill (.btn-text) — used once per section, next to the lead or under the description. Card-internal CTA is a text+arrow link (.btn-link) — used inside cards as a navigation cue because the card itself is already a click target. Putting a pill inside a card competes with the section CTA; putting a text link as the section CTA fails to anchor the section. One tier per role, never both.

Section · default Explore Services .btn-text — bordered pill, 1.5px ink border, 13/24 padding
Section · hover Explore Services Fills ink, arrow slides 3px right
Card · default Explore finance .btn-link — 13px ink, no border, no background
Card · hover Explore finance Color → brand-red, gap widens 6→9px, arrow slides 2px

Rejected variants — do not reintroduce

  • Pill on a card · competes with the section CTA. Cards are already click targets — use .btn-link for the inner cue.
  • Text link as section CTA · loses anchor weight. Section CTA needs the pill border to read as the section's primary call to act.
  • Filled brand-red buttons · red is reserved for page-level CTAs (Post Property, primary nav action) only.
  • Ghost / muted variants · both tiers stay ink-colored. No "Watch on YouTube" red, no muted-grey CTAs.
  • Custom paddings / sizes per section · tiers are canonical — .btn-text is 13/24, .btn-link is 13px text + 12px svg. No per-section tuning.

Rule 06 · Whole-Card Link

The whole card
is the link.

When a card has one primary destination, wrap the entire card in <a> so the full surface is clickable. The "Explore X →" cue at the bottom stays for affordance but becomes a <span> — nested anchors are invalid HTML. Card hover propagates to the inner cue so the link visual updates from anywhere on the surface.

✓ Card is the anchor; inner cue is a span

Entire surface is the click target. The bottom “Explore X →” reads as a link cue, not a separate link.

Either/or, and what pairs with it.

Chips are for a set you scan and compare. A question with two or three fixed answers — buy or rent, this property type or that — is not that set: the answers are known, short, and mutually exclusive, so the control should read as one switch rather than as several competing objects. .seg--choice is the segmented control's paint on radio semantics: a role="radiogroup" of role="radio" keyed off aria-checked, with the chosen surface as a single thumb that slides and resizes to whichever option is on, instead of blinking off in one place and on in another.

styles.css · .seg--choice · main.js · segThumb()
Live · click or use arrow keysRadio semantics · exactly one
  • Segments size to their own label. “Residential” and “Land” are not the same word and do not want the same box; forcing them equal leaves the short one swimming and the long one tight. flex:1 1 auto gives each a content-width basis and shares the remainder evenly, so the difference survives and the row still fits the card at 390 rather than scrolling its last option off the edge.
  • The slide is a clip-path inset on one full-width layer, not a moving box. Variable widths mean a translate alone cannot fit the thumb to its target, and animating the thumb's own width would lay out a box every frame. clip-path stays off the layout path the way transform does, and inset() carries its own round, so the end caps are redrawn at each size instead of being squashed by a scale.
  • The shadow is drop-shadow, not box-shadow, and that is load-bearing. clip-path would cut a box-shadow off at the clip edge; a filter is applied after the clip and traces the shape it actually produced.
  • Positions are measured in px from the OPTIONS, never from the group's scrollWidth. The thumb is absolutely positioned inside the group and so counts toward its scroll overflow: measure the group and the thumb's own width becomes the thing being measured, and the value can then only ever grow. A ResizeObserver and document.fonts.ready re-fit it when the container or the font changes, and mobOpenAcc() re-fits on expand — a group inside a collapsed section has no width to measure.
  • That observer watches the PARENT, and only reacts to a width change. Stacking makes the group taller, which makes its host taller: an observer on the group, or one that reacts to height, feeds itself. The row-or-column decision depends on available width alone, so height changes are noise.
  • Until that first measure the group has no [data-seg-ready]: the thumb stays clipped away, the chosen button paints its own static pill, and the transition is off. The control is readable with no JS and nothing animates in from the wrong place.
  • Semantics are radio, not tab. It reuses the .seg paint but not its meaning: a tablist swaps a panel, this one records an answer. The delegated listener in main.js drives both shapes off one select(), reading aria-checked here and aria-selected there.
  • Selecting fires a native change that bubbles, so whatever owns the answer hears it from the pointer and the keyboard alike. Wiring the setter to an inline onclick instead is how the arrow keys silently stop updating state.
  • The listener runs on capture. A choice group nested in a clickable row has to stop the click reaching that row, and a bubble-phase listener would then never see it at all.
  • When the labels cannot make one row, the control stacks. Not a wrap, which strands a lone segment on its own line and reads as a mistake, and not a scroll, because an option you have to swipe to reach is one most people never see. segFit() asks the question by dropping back to a row first — testing while already stacked always answers “fits”, and the control could never come back. Segments never shrink (flex:1 0 auto) so the overflow it measures is real rather than an artefact of squeezed labels.
  • Stacking is not a second code path for the thumb: it is clipped on all four sides, so a column is just a different set of numbers. The track drops from 999px to 22px + the gutter, since a pill only stays a pill while it is one row tall, and content goes left-aligned — centred labels down a column read as a row that got squeezed.
  • The gutter widens when stacked (--seg-pad 4px → 8px, row gap 5px). A column reads tighter than a row at the same value: side by side the space between two pills doubles as the space around them, while stacked there is a long straight edge down each side with nothing in it. It is a variable rather than a number because the thumb is positioned from it and segThumb() measures against it — hard-coding 4px in three places is how the thumb ends up 4px out the day the padding changes, so the JS reads the computed padding instead of assuming one.
  • Optional .seg__ico, the same idiom as .jm-chip__icon and .jm-tile__icon: a line drawing on currentColor, so it inverts with its label under the thumb and needs no separate rule for the ink variant. Silhouettes have to survive 17px, so each is two or three strokes. A first pass at Land drew a plot with a survey flag and read as a mushroom at that size; the folded map replaced it.
  • A selection animates; a re-measure lands. The layer's height is not a transitioned property, so on a re-fit it jumps while the clip insets are still interpolating — for a beat the thumb covers the whole stack before settling onto one row, which is what expanding a collapsed section used to look like. segThumb(list, false) sets [data-seg-jump] for the write, reads a layout property to commit it, then gives the transition back. Selections keep their slide.
  • Segments clear 44px. The slide is skipped under prefers-reduced-motion; the thumb still lands in the right place, it just does not travel.
  • Labels are the words the search is read in, not the ones the database stores. Residential / Commercial / Land sit over keys that stay homes / workspaces / land, because those map to propertytypeid. One TYPE_LABEL map owns the display strings — the ternary behind them was repeated in six places, where a rename meant six edits and any miss showed two names for one thing on a single screen.

    Chapter 03 · Toasts & Notifications

    Toasts.

    A toast confirms something that succeeded and needs no follow-up: saved, copied, removed. If the user must read it, act on it, or would lose something by missing it, it is not a toast — it is a panel or a dialog. Everything here comes through gharToast(). Never hand-author toast markup.

    01

    Four variants.

    The surface stays ink on every variant and only the icon takes colour, so a stack of toasts reads as one system and none shouts louder than another. Each colour is a theme token chosen because it means that thing, never picked for variety: --sage reads as settled and done, --turmeric is the caution colour everywhere else on the portal, --indigo is the neutral informational tone.     06 — The rules for scaling the voice

    Eight principles.
    If a decision breaks any one of them, the decision is wrong.

    These aren't style guidelines. They're the rules that keep the voice coherent across sixteen sections, six card types, B2C and B2B audiences, and years of future content. When you're three screens deep into a layout decision and can't choose — come back here.

    01
    Warm paper is the canvas.
    Body is #faf7f2 everywhere — product surfaces too. Pure white is a deliberate object that floats on the paper (inner cards, search bars, pills). If a section needs pure white, it's wrong.
    02
    The house is a glyph.
    The nine canonical shapes are typography. They live inside sentences, prefix categories, open quotes, anchor data. Never decoration. Never icons. If a shape isn't doing the work of a letter, cut it.
    03
    Color is vocabulary — earned, not assigned.
    Indigo Mist for Editorial (written ink), Espresso for GharTalks (listening room), Turmeric for GharEvents (ceremony), Ink for VideoWorks (cinema), Terracotta for Community (earth of people). Five earned tones — none repeated, none adjacent. If you can't defend a color choice semantically in one sentence, and the section next to it isn't warm paper, it doesn't belong.
    04
    Stories, not specs.
    Write every line for the person reading, not the business speaking. "5 new listings match your filter" becomes "a new home story starts with five matches." This is not word-play — it's stance.
    05
    The product is a polite guest.
    Search bars sit next to fireplaces. Notifications speak softly. The interface participates in the room — it doesn't yank you out of it. If a component interrupts the mood, rewrite the component.
    06
    Data is typography.
    Big serif stats. Shape-silhouettes as data dots. Histograms, not rainbow pie charts. Intelligence without clinical. Assume the reader can read a chart — don't dumb the data down to marketing.
    07
    B2B earns authority through craft.
    Broker, developer, and partner sections keep the warm voice but swap emotion for precision. Sharper type hierarchy, tighter grids, denser proof. Never colder color — just tighter composition. Atmosphere stays; editorial shifts.
    08
    One voice. Sixteen rooms.
    Variety lives within the voice, not against it. Different sections have different weight, density, color, and rhythm — but the reader should never feel they left Ghar.tv. If a section feels like it belongs to a different brand, it does. Rebuild it.
    A homepage isn't a screen. It's an opening chapter.
    Build every section as if the reader will stop here and read to the end.

    Industry Voices (.vx-*) — claim-led card family.

    Three pages: /voices (landing), /voices/search (browse), /voices/{slug} (piece). Sources: voices.html, voices-search.html, voices-article.html. CSS lives in styles.css (search VOICES CHASSIS). Full contract in docs/VOICES-HANDOFF.md.

    Why a new card family at all. Every layout FUNCTION here is reused (grids, rails via initCarousel, section heads, buttons, tokens, the whole .br-* finder, the whole art-* block catalog). What is new is the card FACE, because the Design card design is wrong for this content. /design cards are image-led — the photograph is the story. /voices cards are claim-led — the sentence is the story, the face is the credential, the source is the proof. Reusing .nr-card would have dragged the wrong design layer along with the right function layer.

    • vx-hero — The landing hero, full viewport (100svh minus the nav stack, minus the 64px bottom bar on mobile). Two blocks, kept separate on purpose: .vx-hero__intro is the PAGE (h1 + description, the SEO surface, static per vertical) and .vx-hero__card is the FEATURED PIECE (rotates). Do not let the piece's copy bleed into the intro, or the page appears to be described by whatever story happens to be leading that week.
    • Card anatomy — .vx-hero__card (white, bordered) holds three children in order: the kicker (THIS WEEK · DEVELOPER DIALOGUES, centred, labelling the piece before the quote it labels), the stage (warm inset holding the ink quote box and the speaker), and the foot (.vx-hero__foot: provenance left, read link right, justified on one line). Same three-surface construction as the homepage Voices card, .vc4-card > .vc4-stage > .vc4-bubble.
    • Quote length: four size tiers. A hero quote is editor-supplied and unpredictable. data-len on the blockquote selects a tier (s ≤80 chars, m ≤140, l ≤210, xl beyond), which the template sets server-side with an inline script recomputing it from the real text as a fallback. All four verified to hold the hero at an exact 754px viewport fit. No truncation, ever: no line-clamp, no ellipsis. Cutting a sentence short misrepresents what a named person said, which is the one thing this vertical cannot do. Past the longest tier the hero grows taller than the viewport, which is the honest failure mode; the fix is editorial, not CSS.
    • Below 744px the box is dropped entirely. Card and stage both lose their background, border, radius and padding, and the content lays out directly on the body. Nested surfaces cost real width on a phone: --pad-h 24 + card 14 + stage 14 was 52px of inset per side on a 390px screen, leaving the bubble at 276px and wrapping the speaker's role onto two lines. Dropping the box takes the bubble to the full 332px. The quote mark hides with it (it needed the frame to read as a mark rather than a stray glyph), the intro takes a bottom rule since the card edge no longer separates it, and the foot stacks with order putting the action above the fine print.
    • vx-source — The signature. Hairline rule + SOURCE small-caps + platform and date + outbound link. Repeats on every surface that carries a claim. A quote without a live source is aggregation; a quote with one is intelligence, so the source is shown rather than buried.
    • vx-speaker — Face + name + role. One component, three sizes (--sm 34px, base 44px, --lg 64px). Portraits use object-position:50% 22% for head-safe cropping.
    • vx-card — Our own conversation or perspective. No card media. Order is eyebrow → claim (Gazpacho, leads) → hairline → speaker credit → format. An earlier cut carried a 4:3 portrait on top, which put the same face on the card twice (once as media, once in the credit row) and made the card about the person. The card is about the argument; the person gains standing because the argument is worth reading, so the claim takes the space the image used to. Do not reintroduce .vx-card__media.
    • vx-quote — Curated from elsewhere. Deliberately text-led with no media: we do not own imagery of that person in that moment. Mark, quote, editorial framing line (.vx-quote__why, never optional), small speaker row, source receipt.
    • vx-sp — The Speakers index. Face, current claim, and their appearances across the portal, each name resolving to /people/{slug}.
    • vx-contribute / vx-door — The supply side. Two intake doors (write a perspective, nominate a voice). An opinion vertical with no commissioning budget is fed by recruitment, not by editorial alone.

    Voice and hashtags

    How the caption sounds

    We sound like someone who knows the market explaining it to a friend who is about to spend a lot of money. Intelligent, plain, never breathless. The caption adds what the frame does not already say; it never repeats the headline back.

    Write this
    Registrations held flat through August, against a monsoon that usually takes ten percent off. The full read is in this month's report. Says something the frame did not. Gives a reason to tap.
    Not this
    🔥 HUGE news! Mumbai registrations are holding STRONG 💪 You won't believe what happens next. Link in bio!! 🏠✨ Emoji as punctuation, a promise the story does not keep, and nothing learned.
    Caption patternThree moves, in order
    • Open with the fact. The most specific true thing, in one sentence. No question openers, no "Did you know".
    • Add the context the frame could not hold. One or two sentences. This is the part worth reading.
    • Point somewhere. One destination, named plainly. "The full report" beats "Link in bio".
    RuleDetail
    LengthUnder 60 words. The first 12 are what most people read
    EmojiNone in the first line. At most one elsewhere, and only if it carries meaning
    CaseSentence case. Never all caps for emphasis
    NumbersLakhs and crores. Sq ft, not sqft. DD/MM dates
    NamesFull name on first mention, role after a middot
    HashtagsThree to five, never a wall

    One pillar tag, one or two topic tags, one city tag when the post is local. Tags are written in camel case so they stay readable.

    #GharTv #GharTalks #GharEvents #GharIntelligence #IndianArchitecture #IndianHomes #RealEstateIndia #MumbaiRealEstate #BengaluruRealEstate
    • #GharTv goes on everything. It is the only tag that is not optional.
    • No engagement bait tags. No #followforfollow, no #viral, no tag unrelated to the post.
    • Tags sit at the end of the caption, never scattered through the sentences.

    Accessibility and disclosure

    The parts that are not taste

    Everything above is craft and can be argued. This section is not. A frame that fails here does not go out, however good it looks.

    LegibilityChecked before export, not after posting
    • Contrast 4.5:1 minimum for any text meant to be read, measured against what is actually behind it. Over a photograph, measure against the lightest patch the type crosses, not the average.
    • Captions on every video. Social plays muted by default, so a video without burned-in subtitles reaches a fraction of its audience and excludes deaf and hard-of-hearing viewers entirely. This is not optional.
    • Alt text on every image post. Describe what is in the frame and what it means, in one sentence. "Courtyard of a Bengaluru home, open to the sky, with a stone floor" beats "image" and beats a list of hashtags.
    • Never colour alone to carry meaning. Pair it with a label, a shape or a position.
    • No flashing faster than three times a second, in any cut. It is a seizure risk.
    • Type below 28px on a 1080 canvas is decoration, not information. Assume nobody reads it.
    Paid and partnered contentLabel it, always

    Anything a brand paid for or supplied carries a label, on the frame itself and in the caption. The platform's own paid-partnership tool is used as well, not instead.

    RelationshipLabelUse when
    Brand funded the piece, we made itIn Collaboration WithEditorial control stayed with us
    Brand owns the piecePresented ByThe brand shaped the content
    Product or stay was giftedGiftedNothing was paid but something was received
    • The label is never smaller than the eyebrow and never sits in a safe band where the platform will cover it.
    • Sponsorship attaches to a card or a piece, never to a section header, a topic strip or the account itself.
    • Photographs carry their credit. A photographer's name is not optional because the frame is small.
    • Quote a person only from something they actually said on record. Verbatim, trimmed with an ellipsis at most.

    The brief, for an AI tool

    Paste this before you ask for anything

    Everything above, compressed into constraints a model can follow. Paste it as a system prompt or at the top of a chat, then make your request. It is deliberately blunt: a flat list of rules produces on-brand output far more reliably than a description of the design philosophy. Re-copy it whenever this chapter changes, since it is maintained by hand.

    Ghar.tv brand brief
    You are producing social media and video content for Ghar.tv. Follow these rules exactly.

BRAND
Ghar.tv is India's real estate discovery, intelligence, media and events platform. It is not a listing portal. Tagline: "Real Estate. For You."
Positioning: understanding-first, not transaction-first. We help people understand the market before they transact.
Personality: intelligent, empowering, trustworthy, editorial, premium, calm.

VOICE
Write like someone who knows the market explaining it to a friend who is about to spend a lot of money.
Plain verbs. Sentence case. Specific over clever. Active voice.
Never: hype, breathlessness, emoji as punctuation, ALL CAPS for emphasis, clickbait, "Did you know", "You won't believe", "Link in bio" as the only direction.
Never use em dashes. Use commas, colons or full stops.
Say "Industry Voices", never "arguments", "takes", "claims" or "hot takes".
Product names stand alone: GharTalks, GharEvents, VideoWorks, Brand Connect, SuperPro, Intelligence.

CAPTIONS
Three moves in order: (1) open with the most specific true fact in one sentence, (2) add the context the image could not hold, (3) point to one destination, named plainly.
Under 60 words. The first 12 carry it. No emoji in the first line, at most one elsewhere and only if it means something.
Hashtags at the end, three to five, camel case. #GharTv on everything. One pillar tag, one or two topic tags, one city tag if local.
Never engagement bait: no #viral, no #followforfollow, no unrelated tags.

NUMBERS AND FORMATS
Indian conventions only. Rupees, and lakhs and crores, never dollars and never "4.2M". Write ₹4.2 Cr.
Dates as DD/MM. Areas as "sq ft".
Never invent a statistic. Every figure names the published report it came from, or it is cut.
Never invent project names, client names, or awards.

COLOUR (hex is exact)
Foundation: White #ffffff, Warm White #faf7f2, Rule #e5dcc8, Sand #e0c199, Muted #6e655b, Espresso #3a2f27, Ink #1a1714.
Brand: Brand Red #ee324b, Rose #f37e86, Blush #f6aaad, Petal #fbd4d5, Mist #fde8e8.
Theme: Marigold #eda41c, Coral #d5613a, Olive #9aa96d, Indigo #5f71a9, Aubergine #895772.
Brand Red is ONLY the logo and one call to action per frame. Roughly 5% of a frame maximum. It is never an eyebrow, hashtag, date, bullet, border, quote mark or decorative dot.
One theme colour per frame, chosen because it matches the subject, not for variety. Do not blend a theme colour with a foundation tone. No two-stop gradient backgrounds.
Text contrast 4.5:1 minimum.

TYPE
Gazpacho Bold: headlines, pull quotes and any number over 14px. Line height 1.08. Default tracking.
Inter: everything else, weights 400 to 700. Body line height 1.6 to 1.8.
Suiza SemiBold: the logo tagline only. Never a heading or body font.
Never set a headline in Inter. Never set body copy in Gazpacho. Never italicise Gazpacho.

LOGO
Always the supplied SVG or PNG file, never typed out in a font.
Clear space equal to the height of the G on all sides. Minimum 24px tall; below that use the G mark alone.
Never beside a shape glyph. Never recoloured, outlined, shadowed or rotated.

SHAPES
Nine canonical house silhouettes, each a variant of the logo G. One per frame. They are design moves, not category icons.
A shape may mask a photograph at display scale only.

IMAGERY
Warm natural light, real interiors and real architecture, nothing generic-stock.
Indian or relatable Asian faces whenever a person appears. If a confirmed Indian face is unavailable, use no face: aerials, skylines, material close-ups.
A named individual appears with their real photograph or not at all.

CANVAS SIZES
Square post 1080x1080. Portrait post 1080x1350 (default for Instagram and Facebook). Story and Reel 1080x1920. Landscape video 1920x1080. Avatar 800x800.
Margin 72px on a 1080 canvas.
On a 1080x1920 vertical frame, keep everything inside the live area: top band 250px and bottom band 320px belong to the platform's interface, side margins 64px. Live area is 952x1350.

VIDEO
Every social cut must work with sound off. Burned-in subtitles, Inter 700 at 48px, white with a soft shadow, never a solid box, two lines maximum, above the bottom safe band.
Lower third: name in Inter 700 at 44px, role beneath in Inter 500 at 30px, white plate at 95% with a 3px Brand Red left rule. This is the one red element in an edit.
Reels and Shorts 15 to 45 seconds. Export H.264, AAC 192kbps 48kHz, audio peaks at -1 dBTP, Rec.709 colour, match the source frame rate.
End card holds 2 seconds: one line, the handle, nothing animated.

CONTENT PILLARS
GharTalks (conversations), Intelligence (data and reports), Architecture & Design (spaces and the people behind them), Industry Voices (written perspectives), GharEvents (the four signature shows), Properties (listings and launches).
Every piece belongs to exactly one pillar, which sets the eyebrow and the tag.

DISCLOSURE
Paid or partnered work is always labelled on the frame and in the caption: "In Collaboration With" when we kept editorial control, "Presented By" when the brand shaped it, "Gifted" when something was received.
Sponsorship attaches to a single piece, never to a section or the account.

ALWAYS
Alt text on every image. Captions on every video. Credit every photographer.

NEVER
Never name a competitor platform or a reference brand in public copy.
Never promise a return, a price movement or an investment outcome.
Never use a stock photo of a person to stand in for a real named individual.
    ⚠
    An AI does not check facts This brief constrains style and format, and it tells a model not to invent figures, but a model will still produce a confident wrong number if pushed. Every statistic, name, date and price in generated copy gets checked against the source before it goes out. The brief makes output on brand; it does not make it true.
