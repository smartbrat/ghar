# People and person profiles on Ghar.tv: what they are

What the People directory and a person's profile page are for, who reads
them, and exactly what content and data exist behind them.

Nothing here describes how either page currently looks, what order
anything appears in, or how a reader moves through it. That is
deliberate. This is the brief, not the answer.

---

## 1. The platform

**Ghar.tv** is an Indian real estate discovery, intelligence, media and
events platform. It is not a listing portal. People come to it to
understand a market, to read and watch, to follow the industry, and to
find the professionals working in it. Its brand line is *Real Estate.
For You.*

Two directory verticals sit inside it, built the same way:

- **Brands**, companies: developers, material brands, proptech,
  financial institutions
- **People**, individuals: architects, interior designers, developers,
  brand leaders, advisors, researchers

Every brand and every person can have a page. This document covers the
People half.

---

## 2. How people get onto the platform

Two routes, and the difference between them is the single biggest thing
shaping these pages.

1. **Editorially curated.** We add someone because we published their
   work, or because they matter to a story we are telling. They never
   asked to be listed and have given us nothing. We hold a name, a
   role, a city and little else.
2. **Brand Connect.** A paid engagement. The person or their firm
   supplies portraits, project photography, biography, figures, contact
   details, credentials. A rich record, and this is the minority.

Both are permanent entries in the same directory and both must be
served equally well. A thin record has to read as a deliberate, finished
short page, never as a rich page with fields missing.

---

## 3. The two pages

**The directory** exists to get a reader from "I need an architect in
Mumbai" to the right person. Most readers arrive without a name in mind,
so it is a browse-and-narrow problem, not a search-results problem. It
has to let a reader narrow by discipline, by city, and by name, and it
has to stay useful when a narrow query returns one person rather than
thirty.

**A person's profile** exists to answer four questions: who is this,
what do they do, are they any good, and how do I reach them.

Two things about the profile that are easy to miss:

- It is read by the person it is about. It is also the page they will
  send to a client, so it has to work as their own professional page
  rather than as our database entry about them.
- It carries two kinds of content that belong to different owners:
  their own work and history, and our published journalism about them.
  A reader needs to be able to tell those apart.

---

## 4. The directory: what it holds

Each person in the directory is represented by:

| | Always present |
|---|---|
| Name | yes |
| Photograph | **no** |
| Two-letter monogram, used when there is no photograph | yes |
| Role and employer | yes |
| City | yes |
| Discipline, one of six | yes |
| Areas of work, up to two | no |
| Marker that the person maintains their own record | no |
| Marker that they are a paying Brand Connect tenant | no |

Two things a reader must be able to do from the directory: open a
person's profile, and start a contact enquiry without leaving.

**The data as it stands today:**

- 31 people, of whom **22 have no photograph**
- Architecture 11, Interior design 5, Development 5, Brand leadership 4,
  Advisory 4, Research 2
- Mumbai 17, Delhi NCR 6, then single entries in Bengaluru, Pune,
  Chennai, Kolkata, Ahmedabad, Puducherry, Alibaug
- Around 70 Indian cities are selectable, with aliases, so "Bombay"
  finds Mumbai and "Bangalore" finds Bengaluru
- Target scale is a few hundred people across about twenty disciplines

The consequences worth holding on to: **roughly two thirds of people
have no photograph**, the disciplines are very unevenly populated, and a
filtered view will frequently return one or two people. Anything that
assumes a face in every slot, or a full set of results, breaks on the
real data.

---

## 5. The profile: the universality rule

**One template serves every person in the directory**, across roughly
twenty professions, whether the record holds four fields or forty. There
is no per-person or per-profession variant.

Only four things can be assumed of everybody:

1. A name
2. A photograph **or** a monogram
3. A role, usually with an employer
4. A city

Everything else is optional, and many records will have none of it. A
person with only those four things has to produce a complete page.
Anything with no data does not appear at all, and nothing renders empty
or with placeholder content.

## 6. The profile: the complete field inventory

| Field | Optional | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Name | no | |
| Photograph | **yes** | A real photograph of that person, or nothing |
| Monogram | no | Two initials, used when there is no photograph |
| Discipline | no | Which of the six directory categories they are filed under. Not their qualification |
| Role | no | Job title |
| Employer | yes | Links through to that company's own page on the platform |
| Affiliation | yes | Used instead of employer for institutions we have no page for |
| City | no | |
| Qualification | yes | "Civil Engineer", "Architect", "Economist" |
| Years in practice | yes | |
| Brief | yes | Two sentences: places the person, says what the work is |
| Specialisations | yes | Up to five named competences, e.g. "Redevelopment", "Liaisoning & statutory approvals", "Feasibility & TDR advisory" |
| Figures | yes | Two to four number-and-label pairs. Real ones: "Since 1982 / Leading the practice", "226 lakh / Sq ft, BDD Chawl redevelopment" |
| Contact and social | yes | Up to five: website, email, LinkedIn, Instagram, YouTube, X, Facebook. Only ever verified URLs |
| Statement | yes | One claim about them, twelve words maximum, written by us |
| Biography | yes | Two or three paragraphs |
| Recognition | yes | Awards, appointments, honours. Awarding body and year, both optional |
| Credentials | yes | Formal registrations as label-and-value pairs: "Registration / Council of Architecture (COA)" |
| Work | yes | Their projects or output. A title, a short line of context, and sometimes a photograph. Photographs are frequently absent |
| Our published coverage | yes | Ghar.tv's own pieces involving them |
| Colleagues | derived | Other people in the directory at the same employer |

**On our published coverage.** These are pieces we made, not pieces they
made, and they come in distinct kinds: features and interviews, podcast
episodes, written opinion pieces, and research reports or rankings. Two
things about them: a large share have no image we own, and a piece can
be sponsored, in which case it has to carry attribution to the sponsor.

**On work.** The word for it comes from the record, because a
researcher's output is not "work", a broker's is not "projects" and a
journalist's is not either. There is no separate page for an individual
project, so a project is a thing to look at and read about, not a thing
to click into.

---

## 7. Every state the profile has to survive

A single template has to cover all of these:

1. A full record: photograph, brief, figures, contact links, statement,
   biography, credentials, recognition, work with photography, published
   coverage, colleagues
2. A sparse record: name, monogram, role, city, one sentence, nothing
   else
3. No photograph
4. No employer, so no colleagues and no company to link to
5. No biography at all
6. A biography with no statement and no recognition beside it
7. Work with photography
8. Work with no photography
9. No work at all
10. Published coverage of one kind only, two items
11. Published coverage of several kinds, a dozen or more items
12. No published coverage at all
13. No contact or social links
14. No figures
15. No specialisations
16. No recognition, which is the ordinary case: most people in a
    directory have never been given an award

## 8. Editorial rules that shape the content

These are not stylistic preferences. They are why so much of the page is
optional, and they cannot be designed around.

- **Nothing is fabricated.** No invented statistics, project names,
  quotations, awards, dates or social handles. A field with no verified
  source stays empty and its block does not appear.
- **A real photograph of the named person, or a typographic monogram.**
  Never a stock face, never an AI-generated face, never a generic
  avatar. Since most people have no photograph, the monogram is the
  normal case rather than the fallback.
- **No quotations.** We hold no verified quotes for these people. The
  statement is our description of them, never words in their mouth.
- **A firm's clients, empanelments and awards belong to the firm's
  page**, not to an individual's.
- **Project photography comes from the person or their firm**, never
  from stock libraries.
- **One directory, one treatment.** Profiles are not themed to the
  person's employer. Most people have no company and no colour of their
  own, and theming the ones who do would make the paying tier visible as
  a caste system inside a directory.
- **No verification badge.** Nearly everyone arriving through Brand
  Connect is confirmed by default, so a badge on almost every page
  carries no information and marks the remainder as doubtful.
- **Indian conventions**: ₹, lakhs and crores, DD/MM dates.
