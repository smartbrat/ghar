1. Why this exists

The earlier /design nav mixed four different axes in one list (Disciplines, People, Formats, Topics). Every new business angle wanted a new slot. The list grew unbounded and couldn't cleanly absorb the long-tail brands we already wanted to cover.

The fix: stop thinking in categories. Think in records + tags. Every surface becomes a saved tag query rendered with editorial layout. This is the pattern Architectural Digest uses at the read layer, and Shopify uses at the commerce layer — and it's what protects the system from category sprawl.

The Shopify parallel that grounds the model:

  • A product carries a single type plus open tags plus typed metafields. It lives at one canonical URL.
  • A smart collection is a saved rule (“tag = blue AND type = shirt”) that auto-populates from any matching product.
  • Storefront filters run the same rules engine as UI on a collection page — tick blue and chinese-collar, view narrows.
  • Tag once, appear everywhere it matches. Discovery multiplies without editorial overhead per surface.

Ghar.tv translation: story = product, pillar = product type, editorial tags = tags, structured attributes = metafields, landing pages = smart collections, chip strips = storefront filters.

4. Page types — 4 max

Every URL under /design is one of four patterns. Landings can multiply infinitely; the page-type count stays at 4.

Page typeURL patternPopulated by
Editorial hub/designCurated editorial + Collections rail + featured brands/people
Pillar landing (7 total)/design/{pillar}Auto: all stories tagged with that pillar
Collection landing (graduated tags)/design/{slug}Auto: all stories tagged with that tag (once tag has graduated per §7)
Individual Story/design/{slug}The canonical article; ONE URL forever

Collections and Stories share the same URL depth (/design/{slug}). That's deliberate — matches AD India, matches Shopify's collection/product parity. What differs is nav promotion (pillars in subnav; collections in the Collections rail on /design).

Slugs are reserved at the CMS layer: story slugs can’t collide with the 7 pillar slugs (architecture, interiors, series, spaces, designers, vastu, guides) or with any graduated collection slug. CMS enforces this on creation.

6. Collections — the cross-cutting lens layer

Collections are cross-cutting saved tag queries with editorial framing. They cut across pillars — a Heritage story might live under Architecture OR Interiors; the Heritage collection surfaces both. Think of them as smart collections in the Shopify sense: define once, populate forever.

Collections live in Act 3 of the 8-act page architecture — right after the hero and subnav on /design, before the long editorial stretch.

Every collection uses ONE tag from the 6 dimensions

A collection landing is defined by a single rule of the form “{dimension}:{value}” — or an editorial group of a few equivalent values. Examples:

CollectionRuleLanding URL
Heritagestyle = heritage/design/heritage
Celebrity Homestag = celebrity-home/design/celebrity-homes
Sustainablediscipline = sustainable/design/sustainable
Small Spacesstyle = compact/design/small-spaces
Awards (India Design 100)tag = india-design-100 (Series)/design/series/india-design-100 or flat /design/awards
First Homeaudience = first-home/design/first-home
Lightingmaterial = lighting/design/lighting
Stone & Claddingmaterial = stone-cladding/design/stone-cladding

Collections are flat siblings to pillars at the URL level, not children. That protects reader mental model (URLs never leak taxonomy details) and matches AD India's actual pattern.

The /design page — on-page rail (8 chips, curated)

The Collections rail on /design shows the 8 most prominent collection lenses at any time — editorial discretion. New collections are added via the “See all collections” modal first; promotion to the rail requires displacing one. The rail caps at 8.

The full inventory lives in the modal. Past ~20 collections, swap the modal for a dedicated /design/collections index page.

7. Graduation rule — when a tag earns its own landing

A tag doesn't auto-generate a landing page. If it did, we'd have 200 thin pages diluting SEO within a year. Graduation is deliberate and criteria-based.

A tag graduates from filter chip to landing page when it satisfies BOTH:

  1. Story count ≥ 10 on that tag (proves editorial momentum — not a one-off angle).
  2. AND either brand-partner interest (someone will sponsor it) OR editorial commitment (we'll keep publishing into it).

Below graduation, the tag exists only as a filter chip on its parent pillar — reachable via /design/{pillar}?tag={slug}. Story pool is real; page dedicated to it is not.

What graduation earns the tag

  • Dedicated landing at /design/{tag-slug} (flat, sibling to pillars)
  • SEO title, meta description, canonical entry (crawlable, indexable)
  • Ad inventory: Featured story (sponsored) + 2 native ad slots + in-stream sponsored article + featured brands module + optional Brand Connect CTA (~8 monetization surfaces total, per /design/heritage template)
  • Entry in the Collections rail on /design (if displacing one)
  • Chip on parent-pillar filter strip switched from ?tag= filter link to direct landing link

Deprecation — what happens if traffic dies

A landing that stops earning its keep collapses back to a filter chip on the parent pillar. Rules:

  • < 3 new stories in 6 months AND no active brand partner → downgrade candidate
  • Downgrade: keep the URL live for 12 months as a 301 redirect to the pillar's ?tag= filter (preserves inbound links + SEO)
  • After 12 months: retire the URL if traffic didn't recover

8. Chip strips — filter + return-path pattern

Chip strips are the storefront-filter equivalent (Shopify parallel). They appear on both pillar landings and collection landings, and serve two jobs in one component:

  • Filter narrowing — reader on a pillar page ticks a tag to narrow the view.
  • Return-path affordance — reader on a collection page sees they're inside a parent pillar's tag family, and can hop to any sibling tag with one click.

Rules for the chip strip on a PILLAR landing

  • Chips = the top 6–10 tags across stories in that pillar, ordered by story count desc
  • First chip is always “All” in .is-active state, linking to the pillar itself
  • Non-graduated tag → chip href = /design/{pillar}?tag={slug} (filter query on same page)
  • Graduated tag → chip href = /design/{tag-slug} (direct link to collection landing)

Rules for the chip strip on a COLLECTION landing

  • Chips = mirror the parent pillar's tag family (same list, same order)
  • The collection's own chip is in .is-active state
  • “All” chip links back to the parent pillar
  • Other tags follow the pillar rules above (graduated → landing; else → ?tag= filter)

Visual state legend

graduated chip links to its own landing at /design/{slug}.
filter chip links to a ?tag= query on the parent pillar.

Count consistency (CMS enforces)

A tag's count on any chip must be its TRUE current story count, and must match wherever that chip appears. Architecture's Heritage 12 chip and Heritage's own Heritage 12 chip both read from the same source. No hand-typed numbers.

9. URL conventions — FINAL (locked 2026-08-01 evening, do not revisit)

Every /design URL is FLAT. One pattern, no branching. `/design/heritage`, `/design/sustainable`, `/design/celebrity-homes`, `/design/mumbai`. Pillars, sub-topics, tags, stories — all at the same depth.

Slug collisions handled by a reserved-slug list at the CMS layer (7 pillar slugs + all graduated tag slugs — a small managed list).

The nested-vs-tag distinction we spent hours debating is an editorial IA decision, not a URL decision. AD has that distinction because they have hundreds of editors on a mature CMS. Ghar.tv has one editor pre-launch. One URL pattern beats two. If you're publishing at Ghar.tv scale a year from now and the flat pattern is causing real problems (not hypothetical ones), reconsider then. Not before.

How adding a new topic works

  1. Editor decides the topic slug (e.g. sustainable).
  2. Reserved-slug list gets updated (add sustainable so no story can be slugged sustainable).
  3. URL is /design/sustainable automatically. Same template as /design/heritage.
  4. Every article tagged sustainable now auto-appears there.

That's the entire flow. No decision tree. No "sub-pillar or tag?" question.

  • Tag slugs live in their own /design/tag/ namespace — can't collide with story slugs.
  • Sub-pillar slugs are per-pillar — /design/architecture/vernacular and /design/interiors/vernacular could both exist if editorially decided.

URL flip-flop log (learn from the churn, don't repeat it):

  1. 2026-06-10 — dimension-namespaced (/design/material/lighting). Wrong.
  2. 2026-07-31 — flat (/design/heritage). RIGHT, but over-explained with a "graduation rule."
  3. 2026-08-01 morning — /design/tag/heritage. Wrong — mimicked AD's rare exception.
  4. 2026-08-01 afternoon — /design/architecture/heritage. Wrong — mimicked AD's IA at wrong scale.
  5. 2026-08-01 evening (FINAL) — back to /design/heritage. Simplest option, matches original instinct, matches user's stated preference throughout. All legacy URLs (/design/architecture/heritage, /design/tag/heritage) kept as aliases.

Lesson: mimicking AD's URL patterns is cargo-cult. AD's shape is a byproduct of their editor count and CMS maturity. Ghar.tv's shape should reflect Ghar.tv's scale. One editor, pre-launch = one URL pattern.

10. Article schema (frontmatter) — the source-side model

Every story carries a structured header. Today this lives as hand-authored HTML on the landings; the schema below defines the shape any future CMS or build script must honor.

--- slug: kerala-heritage-restoration-villa # required, unique, matches URL at /design/{slug} title: "A Kerala Heritage Villa, Restored Grain by Grain" hero-image: "/brand_assets/stories/kerala-villa-hero.webp" published-at: 2026-07-14 updated-at: 2026-07-20 # TIER 1 — pillar (single, required, closed enum) pillar: architecture # TIER 2 — editorial tags (multi, required ≥1, controlled vocab) tags: [heritage, vernacular, restoration] # TIER 3 — structured attributes (typed, optional but each unlocks a discovery surface) city: kochi state: kerala architect: [benny-kuriakose] # person slug — feeds /people/{slug} year: 2024 style: vernacular budget-tier: premium sqft: 8200 brand-partners: [asian-paints, jaquar] # brand slugs — feeds featured-brands module + /brands/{slug} # MONETIZATION OVERLAY (optional) sponsored-by: null # brand slug if paid; null otherwise featured-priority: 0 # 0=normal, 1=lead, 2=hero series-parent: home-tours # if part of a Series franchise ---

One tag or attribute change auto-updates every landing that matches. The editorial workflow becomes “write the story, tag it accurately” — not “write the story, then manually add it to five index pages.”

Which fields become discovery surfaces

FieldSurface it feeds
pillarPillar landing (/design/{pillar}) — auto-listing

city, stateCity page (/{city}) design section + future city landings on /design

Implementation phasing

  1. Phase 1 (now) — hand-authored HTML pages continue. Frontmatter schema exists as a contract; every new story documented in a companion YAML block at the top of its HTML file (as an HTML comment) so future migration to CMS is a lift, not a rewrite.
  2. Phase 2 (when article count ≥ 30) — introduce a JSON manifest (data/stories.json) with all story metadata. Build script consumes it to generate landings + chip strips + counts. Landings stop being hand-rolled.
  3. Phase 3 (when editorial team > 1 person) — headless CMS (Sanity / Payload / Contentful). Same schema shape; adds admin UI + preview + workflow.

We're in Phase 1 today. Phase 2 and 3 are deliberate future decisions, not deferred infrastructure debt.

12. Service-to-design businesses (Studio FOV pattern)

Service-to-design businesses — visualization studios, photographers, model-makers, drafting consultants — are not end-design disciplines, so they don't get a discipline hub. They live as:

  • /brands/{slug} — permanent home, editorial mentions accumulate here
  • /people/{founder} — founder profile
  • Surface across /design via tagged Stories appearing on Series (Studio Visit, Behind the Build) and on Pillar landings that match their work tags
  • Discoverable across other Ghar.tv surfaces: /developers (vendor network), GharTalks, Industry Voices, Brand Connect, GharEvents

13. Sales / media kit (/partner/design)

What your rep shows a prospect (e.g. a model-making studio, anchor switches brand, lighting designer, art gallery). One self-contained page:

  1. Their forever Brand/Person microsite — visual of /brands/{their-name} + /people/{founder}. Owned forever, compounds with every Ghar.tv mention.
  2. Which /design/* surfaces their tags unlock — a tag map showing how their 4–6 tags route them to 8–15 surfaces automatically.
  3. Editorial pathways — Studio Visit · Behind the Build · GharTalks guest · Material Stories feature · India Design 100 candidacy. Programs they can be considered for.
  4. Brand Connect upgrades — VideoWorks production · sponsored Story · featured placement · cross-platform amplification. Capabilities + audience value only, NO internal packaging language.
  5. Single CTA: “Talk to Ghar.tv” → enquiry → marketing team scopes 1:1.

14. The one rule that protects the system

Tag vocabulary is CMS-controlled. Mergeable, deprecatable, aliasable — never free-text.

If we let editors invent tags ad-hoc, we're back to category sprawl in 12 months. This is the one piece of governance that protects the model long-term.

If anyone proposes a 7th object, an 8th tag dimension, a category folder, or a namespaced URL pattern — that's the moment the system starts to rot. AD has held this line for 20 years. Shopify has held it for 20 years across 6M+ merchants. We can too.
