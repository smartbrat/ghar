02 What we make

Six ways a property becomes a story.

Every brief lands somewhere on this list. Most land on more than one, because a launch that only produces a launch film wastes the shoot it already paid for.

01

Brand Campaigns

The film a developer runs for years, not for one quarter. What the company believes, built to outlast the project that paid for it.

  • Developer brand films
  • Corporate vision and legacy films
  • Sustainability and CSR narratives
  • Leadership and culture profiles
02

Product Launches

A launch is a sequence, not a single asset. Teasers that build interest, a reveal that lands it, and cutdowns that keep it alive.

  • Cinematic project reveals
  • Teaser and countdown campaigns
  • Multi-platform launch series
  • Celebrity and influencer shoots
03

Performance Video

Built to the spec each platform actually rewards, and cut to be measured. The brief is a cost per qualified enquiry, not a runtime.

  • Social ads at 15, 30 and 60 seconds
  • Pre-roll and bumper cuts
  • Vertical reels and stories
  • Retargeting sequences
04

Property Films and Tours

The home, and the neighbourhood that decides whether the home is worth it. Shot to be watched, not just to be scrolled past.

  • Cinematic walkthroughs
  • Neighbourhood and locality films
  • Drone and aerial cinematography
  • 360 degree virtual tours
05

Experiential

For buyers who are not in the city, and for inventory that is not built yet. The site visit that happens before the site exists.

  • Virtual reality walkthroughs
  • Augmented reality property tours
  • Interactive 360 degree showcases
  • Virtual event production
06

Documentary

The long view. Buildings take years, and a camera that was there for all of them tells a story no launch film can.

  • Construction time-lapse programmes
  • Architectural documentaries
  • Heritage and restoration films
  • Urban development narratives

04 How we work

The film is the start, not the delivery.

A production house hands over a file. Because the audience and the distribution are ours too, the work keeps going after the edit locks, and what it learns comes back into the next cut.

1

Create

Strategy, script, shoot and edit, in-house. One team from the brief to the master.

2

Distribute

Published across Ghar.tv's editorial network, not handed back for you to find an audience.

3

Amplify

Extended through Brand Connect placements and creator partnerships where it fits the story.

4

Measure

Watch-through, drop-off and enquiry, reported per cut rather than per campaign.

5

Optimise

The cuts that hold attention get more weight. The ones that lose it get recut, not defended.

6

Convert

Enquiries route to your team with the film that produced them attached.

Then it starts again. What step six learns is what step one uses next time, which is the part a studio without an audience of its own cannot do.

05 Why this studio

Four things that usually come from four suppliers.

Marketing a project normally means an agency for the idea, a production house for the shoot, a media buyer for the reach and someone else entirely to explain what happened. That is four briefs and four versions of the story.

01

The category is the specialism

Carpet against built-up, RERA language, what a micro-market will bear, why the approach road matters more than the lobby. A generalist crew learns this on your budget.

02

The audience is already here

Ghar.tv is where people come to understand property before they buy it. A film made here is published to that audience directly, instead of starting from zero on a channel you have to build.

03

Strategy sits in the same room as the camera

The people deciding what the film should argue are the people shooting it. Nothing is lost in the handover between the agency that pitched it and the crew that made it.

04

Results come back as edits

Performance data is only useful if someone can act on it. Here the team reading the numbers is the team that can recut the film that produced them.

07 Questions

The things people ask first.

Has VideoWorks shipped work we can watch?

Not yet. The studio is new and there is no client reel to show, which is why nothing on this page is presented as one. The footage in the header is atmosphere, not a VideoWorks production. We would rather tell you that than pass off stock as a portfolio, and you can judge the craft from the treatment we write for your brief.

Who is this for?

Developers marketing a project or a company, architecture and design practices building a public profile, and allied brands in materials, finance and proptech who sell into real estate. If your audience is people making decisions about property, this is built for you.

Do we have to publish on Ghar.tv?

No. Distribution across our network is the part that is hard to buy anywhere else, so most briefs use it, but the films are yours and run wherever you want them. Production and distribution are scoped separately and you can take either on its own.

Who owns the footage?

You do. Graded masters, the deliverable set and the project files are handed over at the end of the engagement, along with the stills shot on the same set. Usage terms for any licensed music, talent or aerial permits are written into the scope before the shoot, not after it.

How long does a project take?

It depends on the format, and we will give you a real schedule with the treatment rather than a number on a web page. A single interview cut and a multi-city launch series are not the same job, and the honest answer to a timeline question is one that has seen the brief.

What does it cost?

Scoped per brief, because a drone package and a documentary programme share almost nothing. Tell us the format, the city and roughly what you have set aside, and you will get a costed treatment against it. If the budget does not fit the ambition we will say so early rather than at the shoot.

Can you work with our existing agency?

Yes, and it happens often. Plenty of briefs arrive with the campaign idea already settled and need the production and the distribution rather than the strategy. We are equally happy taking a locked concept and executing it.
