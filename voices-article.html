Design Conversations · Interview

“Climate isn’t a feature you add at the end. It decides the plan.”: Sonali Rastogi

The Morphogenesis founding partner on designing for 45°C before designing for looks, why the energy code is the easy part, and what Indian practices keep getting wrong about passive cooling.

Sumera Bhatia · 4 August 2026 · 14 min read
In conversation with Sonali Rastogi Founding Partner, Morphogenesis · Delhi NCR
Part 4 of 9 in Design Conversations

The short version

  • Orientation and massing decide most of a building's energy load, and both are fixed in the first week of design.
  • Compliance codes measure the specification, not the building as operated. The gap between the two is where performance is lost.
  • Passive cooling is treated as a style in India when it is a set of measurements.
  • Clients will pay for comfort. They will not pay for sustainability as an abstraction. The pitch has to change.
In this conversation

    Morphogenesis has spent twenty-five years showing that a building in Delhi should be designed for Delhi. That sounds obvious. It has not been the default. For most of the period in which Indian cities built their current office stock, the reference model was a sealed glass box designed for a temperate climate and then air-conditioned into submission.

    Sonali Rastogi co-founded the practice with Manit Rastogi in 1996. Its work is unusually well documented in performance terms, which makes her a useful person to ask a question the sector mostly answers with adjectives: what actually reduces the energy a building uses, and at what point in the process is it decided?

    We met at the studio in Delhi. The conversation has been edited for length and clarity.

    Where the energy decision actually gets made

    SBIf you had to name the single decision that most affects how much energy a building uses, what is it?

    SRWhich way it faces, and how deep it is. That is two decisions, but they are made in the same afternoon and they are almost never revisited. By the time anyone is choosing glass, the building has already decided how much it will consume.

    SBThat afternoon usually happens before a sustainability consultant is appointed.

    SRAlways. The consultant arrives to certify a decision that was taken months earlier by people who were thinking about saleable area. Then everyone spends the rest of the project buying equipment to correct for it. That is the whole story of the last two decades of Indian commercial building.

    SBIs that a design failure or a procurement failure?

    SRProcurement, mostly. Architects get appointed after the massing is locked into the financial model. If you want a different building you have to change who is in the room in month one, not month nine.

    Sonali Rastogi Founding Partner · Morphogenesis

    “Climate isn’t a feature you add at the end. It decides the plan.”

    The gap between the code and the building

    India's Energy Conservation Building Code has been in force in some form since 2007, adopted unevenly across states. Rastogi's objection is not that it is weak. It is that it measures the wrong moment.

    SBYou have said before that the code checks the drawing, not the building.

    SRIt checks the specification at the point of approval. Nobody comes back in year three to see whether the shading devices were installed, whether the sensors were commissioned, whether the facility manager has simply overridden the whole system because someone complained about a draught. We write energy codes for buildings we haven’t learned to maintain yet.

    SBWhat would close that gap?

    SRPublished operating data. Not certification at handover, consumption at year three, in public. The moment a developer’s buildings can be compared on what they actually use, the incentive changes overnight.

    Editor’s note

    Post-occupancy energy data is not currently published for most Indian commercial buildings. Where operating figures exist they are typically held by the developer or the certifying body and released selectively. We have not independently verified the year-three consumption claim above.

    Passive cooling is a measurement, not a look

    The vocabulary of climate-responsive design has been absorbed into marketing faster than into practice. Jaali screens, courtyards and deep verandahs appear in renders far more often than they appear in commissioned buildings with the geometry that would make them work.

    SBIs the vernacular revival helping or is it decoration?

    SRBoth, and the difference is whether anyone calculated anything. A jaali at the wrong depth on the wrong facade is a pattern. A jaali sized against the sun path for that latitude is a cooling device. They look similar in a render and they perform nothing alike.

    SBHow do you sell the calculated version to a client who wants the render?

    SRYou stop selling sustainability. Nobody buys it. You sell comfort, and you sell the electricity bill. Tell someone their building will be pleasant in May without running the chillers flat out and you have their attention. Tell them it is sustainable and they nod and move on.

    What she would change first

    We finished on the question we put to everyone in this series: one change, available now, that would move the sector further than anything else.

    SBOne change.

    SRBring the architect into the feasibility stage and pay them for it. Not as a courtesy. As the stage where the money is actually decided. Everything else we have talked about follows from that one procurement change.

    SBHow do you know when a room is finished?

    BJYou don’t. You live in it. After a year you know whether the doorway is in the right place. After two, you know whether the height of the bench is right. The room finishes itself.

    SBAnd if you decide it isn’t right?

    BJThen the masons come back. Stone is not a commitment in the way a developer thinks of it. It is patient.

    The traditional approach

    Stone and lime

    Built by hand over months. Thermal mass cools interiors naturally. Repairable in place; ages without falling apart.

    vs
    The modern alternative

    Concrete block + foam

    Faster to build. Relies on mechanical cooling. Shorter lifespan; harder to maintain; produces more embodied carbon.
