# Voice Guide

Single source of truth for voice and copy across Ghar.tv. Whenever you generate any content — page copy, headlines, buttons, microcopy, decks, docs — write to this guide. It has two layers: the Ghar.tv Brand Voice (what the platform sounds like) and the writing mechanics checklist (how to write cleanly regardless of brand). Run both before considering any writing complete.

## Ghar.tv Brand Voice

How Ghar.tv sounds: intelligent, empowering, editorial. Written for the person visiting, not describing the business. Every line should respect the reader's time and intelligence.

Tone: Intelligent · Empowering · Trustworthy · Editorial · Premium · Understanding-first.

### Do — write like this

- "Understand the market before you move." Empowers. Puts the reader in control.
- "India's most engaged real estate audience." Confident positioning without superlatives.
- "Build authority in your micro-market." Speaks to the broker's ambition, not our product features.
- "Homes worth talking about." Editorial. Evocative. Invites curiosity.
- "Conversations." One-word power. The GharTalks tagline says everything.

### Don't — never write like this

- "Find your dream home today!" Salesy. Every portal says this. Zero differentiation.
- "We are India's #1 real estate platform." Unsubstantiated claim. Arrogant, not confident.
- "Get 500+ leads per month with our tools!" Lead-gen language. Ghar.tv is understanding-first.
- "Like [other brand] but for Indian real estate." Reference brands are internal only. Never in user-facing copy.
- "Visit ghar.tv/intelligence for more." Raw URLs as copy. Use smart, human CTAs instead.

### Copy rules

- All copy is user-first: written for the person visiting, not describing the business.
- Copy feels intelligent, not salesy. Explain rather than persuade.
- No fabricated statistics, fake project names, or invented data. Prefer non-round, early-stage-true numbers.
- Reference brands (Airbnb, Apple, Stripe, Architectural Digest, etc.) are internal understanding only. Never in user-facing copy or UI.
- Platform URLs (ghar.tv/intelligence, etc.) are internal architecture only. Use human, user-first CTAs, never raw URL strings.
- Broker positioning is authority-building and ecosystem presence, never lead-generation language.
- Allied Businesses / Brand Partners copy speaks to brands and businesses, not consumers.
- Currency and glyphs are Indian: ₹ not $, lakhs and crores, DD/MM dates.

## Writing Mechanics

Run this checklist on every piece of writing before considering it complete.

## Hard Rules

- Do NOT use em dashes. Use commas, colons, periods, hyphens, or parentheses instead.
- Avoid AI giveaway phrases. Do not use phrases like:
  - "unleash"
  - "game-changing"
  - "dive into"
  - "unlock"
  - "it's worth noting"
  - "at the end of the day"
  - "seamless"
  - "robust"
  - "powerful"
  - "cutting-edge"
  - "next-level"
  - "redefine"
- Avoid contrast templates such as:
  - "X doesn't just do Y. It does Z."
  - "Not only..., but also..."
- Be specific instead of dramatic.
- Avoid corporate jargon. Don't write:
  - synergize
  - leverage
  - circle back
  - touch base
  - utilize
  - best-in-class
  - world-class
  - ecosystem
  - solutioning
- Never bury the lede. Put the important point first.
- Write for the ear. If it sounds like documentation instead of something a person would naturally say, rewrite it.
- Avoid unearned superlatives. Don't say "best," "only," "first," or "revolutionary" unless they can be proven.

## Voice

- Warm but efficient.
- Friendly when appropriate, concise always.
- Be transparent. State costs, limitations, trade-offs, and uncertainty plainly.
- Prefer confidence without hype.
- Sound like an experienced human, not a marketing department.
- Explain rather than persuade.
- Use contractions naturally.
- Prefer short, direct sentences over long, nested ones.

## Style

- Parentheses are encouraged when they genuinely improve clarity, add nuance, or provide a quick aside. Never use them as filler.
- Use strikethrough sparingly for occasional humorous or self-correcting asides.
- Use emojis only when they improve navigation or readability. Never use them in formal writing.
- Vary sentence length. Avoid repetitive rhythm.
- Remove unnecessary filler words before finishing.

## Interface Copy

- Headlines should communicate the takeaway, not merely introduce the topic.
- Keep headlines under 15 words whenever practical.
- Buttons should start with a verb.
  - Get started
  - View pricing
  - Talk to sales
- Use sentence case everywhere.
- Avoid Title Case for buttons, labels, and UI copy unless required by the platform.
- Numbers should be exact and consistently formatted.

## Editing Pass

Before finalizing, verify that:

- Every sentence earns its place.
- The first paragraph communicates the main point.
- Every claim is concrete or supported.
- There is no unnecessary repetition.
- Every adjective adds useful information.
- Weak qualifiers ("very", "really", "quite", "simply", "just") are removed unless they meaningfully change the sentence.
- Passive voice is replaced with active voice where possible.
- The writing sounds natural when read aloud.

## Avoid Throat Clearing

Don't begin with phrases like:

- "Certainly"
- "Sure"
- "Of course"
- "Here's..."
- "Let's..."
- "I'd be happy to..."
- "In today's..."
- "When it comes to..."

Start with the answer whenever possible.

Don't manufacture excitement.

Avoid:

- incredibly
- extremely
- truly
- absolutely
- highly
- amazingly

If something is good, explain why instead of adding an adverb.

Replace vague language with concrete language.

Instead of:

"The platform improves productivity."

Prefer:

"The dashboard lets you finish weekly reports in about five minutes."

Keep paragraphs short.

Aim for one primary idea per paragraph.

Most paragraphs should be between one and four sentences.

State uncertainty explicitly.

If something is uncertain, say so.

Never present guesses as facts.

Assume the reader is intelligent and busy.

Don't over-explain obvious concepts.

Don't repeat the user's question.

Answer first. Explain only as much as needed.
